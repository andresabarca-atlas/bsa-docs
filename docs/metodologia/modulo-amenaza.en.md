# Hazard Module

The BSA 2.0 hazard module characterizes the natural hazards relevant to road infrastructure, prioritizing those that generate recurring disruptions or significant structural damage. The tool works with raster intensity grids associated with discrete return periods, which is the most commonly available data type in Latin American and Caribbean countries.

## Implemented hazards

### Fluvial flooding

Generated by riverbank overflow as a result of prolonged or intense rainfall over a watershed. The approach combines:

- **Hydrological modeling** (estimation of design discharges): tools such as HEC-HMS, HydroBID, or SWAT.
- **Hydraulic modeling** (flow propagation): tools such as HEC-RAS 2D, LISFLOOD-FP, or HydroBID Flood.

The products are maps of **water depth (TH)** and **mean flow velocity (V)** for different return periods.

### Pluvial and coastal flooding

**Pluvial**: originates from intense rainfall that exceeds the capacity of urban drainage systems. Modeled through urban hydrological and hydrodynamic approaches (EPA SWMM, InfoWorks ICM, MIKE URBAN+).

**Coastal**: associated with the combined action of waves, astronomical tide, storm surge, and sea level rise. Represented with models such as ADCIRC, Delft3D, MIKE 21, or XBeach.

Both hazards produce TH and V grids by return period.

### Earthquake

Seismic hazard is represented through **Probabilistic Seismic Hazard Analysis (PSHA)**, integrating seismogenic sources, attenuation laws, and historical catalogs. The products are maps of **Peak Ground Acceleration (PGA)** for different return periods. Tools such as OpenQuake or CRISIS allow regional or national modeling.

### Tsunami

Tsunamis are modeled from coastal and submarine seismogenic sources, propagating waves to the coast using numerical models. The products are grids of inundation height and flow velocity by design event.

### Liquefaction

Liquefaction is the phenomenon whereby saturated soils lose resistance during an earthquake. It is modeled from soil susceptibility maps, seismic acceleration, and water table level. The products are susceptibility indices or probability maps by seismic return period.

---

!!! note "Hazards in future phases"
    The hazards of **landslides** and **hurricanes** are contemplated in the BSA 2.0 design, but are planned for future development phases. They are not implemented in the current version of the tool.

## Temporal and spatial scales

Hazard models are designed to operate from regional to national level, covering extensive road networks and strategic corridors. In terms of temporal scale, the module uses **representative return periods**: 10, 50, 100, 200, and 500 years (or their equivalents in annual exceedance frequency).

The module is compatible with hazard data from international initiatives such as:

- **GLOFAS** (global fluvial flooding)
- **AQUEDUCT** (water risk)
- **ThinkHazard!** (multiple hazards)
- **World Bank Disaster Risk Data Platform**

This structure captures the probabilistic behavior of hazards and is consistent with the simplified estimation approach for EAL and LEC.

## Climate change scenarios

The hazard module allows the incorporation of **future climate scenarios**, particularly the *Shared Socioeconomic Pathways* (SSP) from CMIP6:

- **SSP2-4.5**: intermediate emissions scenario.
- **SSP5-8.5**: high emissions scenario.

Hazard maps generated with precipitation or sea level projections under these scenarios can be included, enabling the **comparison between current and future scenarios** to capture the incremental effect of climate change on hazard. This is consistent with the CMIP5 and CMIP6 General Circulation Models (Eyring et al., 2016).

!!! tip "Interoperability with existing data"
    The tool accepts hazard grids in GeoTIFF and Esri Raster Grid formats, facilitating the use of hazard models already generated by national agencies or international institutions, without the need to run new hydrodynamic modeling.

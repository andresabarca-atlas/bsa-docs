    Concept Glossary¶

    Definitions of technical terms used in the BSA 2.0 methodology and documentation. Listed alphabetically.

    Source: BSA 2.0 Concept Report glossary.

    Concept Definition
    Disaster Result of the manifestation of one or more natural or unintentional anthropogenic events that, upon encountering conditions of vulnerability in people, assets, infrastructure, livelihoods, provision of services, or environmental resources, cause human, material, economic, or environmental damage or losses, generating intense and widespread disruption to the normal functioning of society.
    Exceedance Frequency Annual probability of a given event magnitude being equaled or exceeded in a given year.
    Exposure (Exposed elements) Presence of people, livelihoods, environmental services, economic and social resources, cultural assets, and infrastructure that, due to their location, can be affected by the manifestation of a hazard.
    Forcing External forces acting on a simulated system. At large scales, it may refer to climatological and meteorological forcing; at regional and local scales, to hydrological forcing such as precipitation or peak flows for a given time period.
    Frequency of Annual Exceedance Temporal probability measure obtained as the inverse of the return period. Indicates the probability with which a given flood intensity will be equaled or exceeded in a given year.
    Frequency of Annual Occurrence Number of times per year that a given event is expected to occur.
    Hazard Latent danger that a physical event of natural origin, caused or accidentally induced by human action, will occur with sufficient severity to cause loss of life, injuries, or other health impacts, as well as damage to and loss of assets, infrastructure, livelihoods, service provision, and environmental resources.
    Hydrometeorological hazards Process or phenomenon of atmospheric, hydrological, or oceanographic origin that can cause death, injuries, or other health impacts, property damage, loss of livelihoods and services, social and economic disruption, or environmental damage.
    Hydrodynamic model Technical procedure that reproduces the movement or dynamics of surface water in a watershed using physical and/or mathematical models. Its primary objective is to estimate the extent and magnitude of flood events from flood hydrographs.
    Hydrograph Graphical representation of the variation of streamflow over time, produced as a result of a particular precipitation event.
    Hydrological analysis Study associated with hydrological modeling for estimating flood flows, representing a simplified process of the water cycle in a watershed. Conducted to manage floods and droughts, or to provide data supporting decision-making in integrated watershed management.
    Hyetograph Graphical expression of the variation of precipitation intensity or depth as a function of storm duration, discretized into defined time steps.
    Iso-frequency Concept used in hydrological/hydraulic modeling for estimating flood zones associated with a single return period, assumed to be the same as that of the triggering event.
    Loss Quantity associated with great uncertainty, arising from various sources of hazard and vulnerability components. Due to its random nature, it is treated as a variable described by a probability distribution, conditioned on the occurrence of an event.
    Loss Exceedance Curve (LEC) Curve that specifies the annual frequencies with which events are expected to occur in the future that exceed a determined value of losses for the analyzed portfolio of exposed elements. Allows calculating the Expected Annual Loss (EAL) as the area under the curve.
    Probability Field of knowledge that quantifies all possible outcomes of an experiment in which uncertainty or randomness is present.
    Random experiment Any experiment whose result is conditioned by chance and uncertainty. It can be repeated indefinitely under the same conditions; its possible results are known in advance, but a specific result cannot be predicted with certainty in a particular realization.
    Random variable Variable that can take numerical values associated with the possible outcomes of a random experiment.
    Resilience of a system Capacity of a system to return to its original situation when an event disturbs it (e.g., a natural hazard or a human-caused interruption).
    Return period (Tr) Also known as recurrence period or interval. Mean time (in years) between independent events of equal or greater magnitude than a defined threshold. It is the inverse of the annual exceedance frequency.
    Risk analysis and assessment Process involving consideration of the causes and sources of risk, its consequences, and the probability of occurrence. Relates hazard and vulnerability of exposed elements to determine possible social, economic, and environmental effects and their probabilities, for the purpose of defining interventions and scope of risk reduction.
    Risk management Social process of planning, execution, monitoring, and evaluation of permanent policies and actions for the knowledge of risk, prevention of its generation, reduction or control where it already exists, preparation for disaster situations and subsequent recovery. Its purpose is to contribute to people's safety, well-being, and quality of life and to sustainable development.
    Sample space Set of all possible known outcomes of a random experiment.
    Stationarity In time series analysis, property of a stochastic process whose probability distribution at a fixed instant is the same for all instants in time. Implies that parameters such as mean and variance do not vary over time.
    Vulnerability Susceptibility or physical, economic, social, environmental, or institutional fragility of a community or system to be affected by a dangerous physical event. Corresponds to the predisposition to suffer losses or damage to people, their livelihoods, and their physical, social, economic, and support systems.
    Water Depth (TH) Flood depth, expressed in meters. Hazard intensity parameter used in BSA 2.0 vulnerability functions.
